BusinessOS helps small shops and traders turn WhatsApp messages and voice notes
into orders and a customer ledger (khata). A business owner signs up for BusinessOS;
that owner's customers and suppliers message the owner's WhatsApp Business number.

## What we collect

- **WhatsApp phone numbers and display names** of the people who message a business
  that uses BusinessOS.
- **Message text** sent to that business's WhatsApp number.
- **Voice notes.** We send the audio to a speech-to-text service to get a transcript.
  We store the transcript. We do not keep the audio file.
- **Order and payment details** extracted from messages: items, quantities, amounts,
  delivery dates, payment status, and the business owner's confirmations.

## How we use it

- To understand each message, propose an order or payment entry, and ask the business
  owner to confirm it before anything is saved.
- To keep the business's order history and customer ledger.
- To answer questions the business owner asks about their own business data.
- To send the owner reminders and daily summaries.

We do not sell personal data. We do not use it for advertising.

## Who processes it

We use these providers to run the service:

- **Meta (WhatsApp Cloud API)** to send and receive WhatsApp messages.
- **OpenAI** to transcribe voice notes and to understand message text.
- **Supabase** to store data in a database.
- **Upstash** to queue messages while they are processed.

Each provider handles data under its own terms. We do not log the raw content of
messages in our server logs.

## Who can see it

Data belongs to the business that uses BusinessOS. Only that business owner (and us,
as needed to run and fix the service) can see its messages, orders and ledger. One
business cannot see another's data.

## Retention and deletion

We keep a business's data while it uses BusinessOS. A business owner or a person

## Security

Webhook requests from Meta are verified with a signature before we process them.
Access keys are kept outside the code. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot
promise absolute security.

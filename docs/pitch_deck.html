The problem

The owner is the system.

A small shopkeeper takes dozens of orders a day — raw material from vendors, goods to customers — half by phone call, half on WhatsApp. The udhaar lives in a paper diary. The stock lives in his head. There is no ERP, no CRM, no ops tool — there never was. Every forgotten order is a lost sale; every un-chased payment is cash left on the table.

"Itne saare log call karke order dete hain — sab yaad rakhna mushkil ho jaata hai."
— a kirana owner, unprompted. "So many people call to order; keeping track of it all is hard." He asked us to build exactly this.

The idea underneath

Everything is an order
with a direction.

One simple model powers the whole system — and it means there's no separate inventory tool to build or maintain. Stock, the khata, "what sold vs what I bought" are all computed from the two streams of orders.

PURCHASE vendor → you "50 kg cheeni bhej do" stock +50, a payable
SALE customer → you "2 kg cheeni" stock −2, a receivable
────────────────────────────────────────────
current stock = Σ purchases − Σ sales (derived, never counted)
who owes me = Σ unpaid sales per contact (the khata)

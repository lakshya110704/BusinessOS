# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Real wedge into a small, definable market.** Kirana shops in one neighbourhood cluster is a small market that can plausibly be dominated first, which is what the Monopoly lens wants. The cluster-by-cluster go-to-market matches it.
- **Genuine secret, if it holds.** The founder's bet is that ledger apps fail because they demand typing, and that capturing how owners already talk (WhatsApp, Hindi voice) removes the work. That is a specific, non-obvious claim about why Khatabook and OkCredit accounts go stale. It has not yet been tested with retained users.
- **A durable-asset candidate exists.** Per-shop memory (counterparties, balances, normalized item list, the owner's phrasing) can become switching cost and, later, a proprietary order-history dataset that underwrites credit. Credit underwritten on verified order flow is the one place a real proprietary advantage could form.
- **Timing is defensible.** Hindi/Hinglish voice transcription and cheap LLM extraction are only now good enough and cheap enough for this, and the customer already lives on WhatsApp. No new app or number lowers adoption friction.
- **The capture loop is demonstrated end to end** on real infrastructure, so the engineering risk of the core idea is lower than for a pure concept.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage yet; the "moat" is a data-accumulation claim, not a built asset.** The models are rented and the WhatsApp pipeline is reproducible by a competent team in weeks. The memory only becomes a moat after real shops use it for months, and today there are zero active or paying shops, the DB was wiped, and no retention data exists. In month six, a copycat, or Khatabook/OkCredit adding a WhatsApp or voice capture mode, can match the product; the incumbents already have 50M+ installs and the distribution. The lens finds nothing here that is ten times better on something that matters, only proven demand for the problem.
2. **Platform dependency on Meta, with distribution unsolved.** The product lives entirely inside a channel the company does not control: Business Verification, Live-mode approval, and pre-approved templates for proactive messages are all unresolved, and Meta can change pricing or policy. Separately, the brief gives a go-to-market style (in-person, one cluster at a time) but no evidence of how the first 100 shops arrive or what each costs. Founder-led, in-person sales to kirana owners is slow and linear, not a distribution advantage.
3. **The money sits on a different business than the one being built.** The product is free; revenue is meant to come from collections fees, NBFC lending spread, and network take-rate. Lending and a two-sided network are each separate, harder businesses, and the network effect (a verified buyer-supplier graph) is asserted, not designed: nothing in the brief shows why one shop's use makes it more valuable to another. Without a network effect or scale advantage, this is a competitive utility fighting on thin margins. Also open: the headline "ask your business anything" feature, call ingestion, and derived stock are not built, so the differentiated promise is mostly future.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret, in one sentence, that you know and Khatabook does not? What evidence beyond one unprompted shopkeeper quote supports it?
- What happens in month six when Khatabook or OkCredit ships WhatsApp or voice capture? Which part of your product do they not have, and why can they not copy it?
- Which single cluster do you own first, how many target shops is it, and what share do you need before it is hard to dislodge? (Market size and density are unknown; /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer should size this.)
- How do the first 100 shops arrive, and what does each cost to acquire and to serve per month? (/founder-cfo for CAC, API cost per shop, and price.)
- Why would a shop use this every morning after week three? What is the retention evidence from even five real shops?
- Where exactly is the network effect: why does shop A using it make shop B's experience better, and why would a supplier join?
- What is your plan if Meta delays verification, restricts the use case, or raises message costs?
- Who is the team, and what makes them the right people to sell to kirana owners and later run a lending partnership?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- **Prove retention before scale.** A pilot of real shops in one cluster, with evidence that owners are still confirming orders and asking questions daily after several weeks. Without it, the memory moat does not exist.
- **Show a named, buildable advantage** (own the order-history data and underwriting, or a concrete network mechanic) beyond rented models and a WhatsApp wrapper, with a written answer on the incumbent response.
- **Close the platform risk:** Business Verification and Live mode approved, templates working, and a fallback channel or plan.
- **Deliver the unit numbers** (price or revenue path, CAC, cost to serve, cluster size) via /founder-cfo, /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer, and name the team.
- **Tie the money to the product:** a first revenue stream with an actual timeline, not three future businesses.

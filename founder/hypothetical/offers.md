# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Pain is named and plausible: dozens of orders a day, half by phone, udhaar in a paper diary, stock in the owner's head. One kirana owner reportedly asked for this unprompted (a single anecdote, not proof).
- Effort is the value-equation term where the offer is best: no new app, no new number, no typing, owner confirms with a tap. This directly answers why ledger apps go stale.
- Time to value looks short: the first captured order can produce a visible result the same day (assuming the hypothetical build works as stated).
- The stack is coherent and cheap to deliver per piece: capture, khata, derived stock, ask-in-Hindi, collections. Collections (udhaar recovered) ties the offer to money the owner already cares about.
- Free operations layer removes price as the objection at the point of entry.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is unproven, and the market score is unknown.** Zero shops, zero retention data, no proof that a shopkeeper keeps using it past week two. The dream outcome ("run my day from here") is large, but the buyer has no reason to believe it works and the offer has no guarantee. Buyer money is also unproven: kirana owners are cash-tight, and nothing shows they will pay anyone.
2. **Free product, monetization from someone else's money, with no numbers.** The revenue comes from a fee on udhaar recovered, NBFC lending spread and a take-rate on a network that does not exist yet. All three depend on scale and trust the business has not earned. No price, no cost-to-serve (AI and voice per shop per month), no CAC. Free can also mean the owner places no value on it, which hurts retention.
3. **Effort and sacrifice are understated.** The owner must get customers and vendors to message the business, take calls that must be recorded, and confirm every order. Collections reminders put the owner's customer relationship at risk (the founder's own argument is that trade is a relationship). If confirm taps or recording become a chore, the effort term erases the zero-typing promise. Reach is also open: in-person, one cluster at a time is slow and expensive, and cluster density is unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the first buyer: which shop type, which city, how many orders a day, and what do they pay today to solve this? (/founder-consumer)
- What does one shop pay, or what does it save or recover, per month? What is the price that still makes this the obvious choice? Open number. (/founder-cfo)
- What does serving one shop cost per month in AI, voice and support, and what does acquiring one cost in person? Open numbers. (/founder-cfo)
- How many target shops exist, how dense is a cluster, and is the market growing? Open number. (/founder-competitors)
- What do Khatabook, OkCredit and the AI call-bots offer today, and what would make a shop switch rather than add one more tool? (/founder-competitors)
- What proof will a new shop see before it trusts this, and what is the guarantee if it does not work in the first two weeks? (/founder-consumer)
- What is the owner's real time and effort per day, including confirmations and call recording? (/founder-consumer)
- What does the owner lose by leaving after six months, and does the owner feel it? (/founder-consumer)
- Who is on the team, and who has sold into kirana shops before? Open question (outside this lens's scoring of the business).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Run a pilot with real shops (a named number, set before it starts) and show daily-use retention at week 4 and week 8, plus orders captured per shop per day.
- Show at least one revenue path with real money in it (for example, udhaar recovered with a fee actually collected) before any lending or network take-rate story is credited.
- Publish a price or fee and a per-shop cost-to-serve, with a positive margin on the paid path.
- Put a written guarantee in front of the buyer (for example, results in the first two weeks or no charge on collections) and show it holds in the pilot.
- Produce a measured CAC for in-person acquisition of one cluster and a cluster size that makes the 18-month plan reachable.

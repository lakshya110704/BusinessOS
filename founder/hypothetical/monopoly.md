# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small, definable starting market.** Kirana and similar neighbourhood shops, entered one cluster at a time, is the right shape for the Monopoly lens: win a small pond fully, then expand. The founder's plan to go in person, cluster by cluster, fits that.
- **A real secret, if it is true.** Ledger apps go stale because they make the owner type. The claim that capturing how the owner already talks (WhatsApp, Hindi voice, calls) removes that work is a non-obvious insight. It is the founder's claim and is untested.
- **The only plausible durable asset is a data and switching-cost one.** Each shop's accumulated memory (vendors, customers, balances, item list, speech patterns) could become a switching cost, and a verified buyer-supplier network could become a network effect. These are the two durability routes this lens asks for, and the brief names both.
- **Money sits in the flow, not the software.** Free operations layer, paid collections, credit and take-rate means the business earns where the data is most valuable. That points at a position that is hard to dislodge, if it works.
- **The product is built and the hard platform gates are cleared** (verification, Live mode, templates). Timing is plausible: voice models are now good enough for shop speech.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No 10x, and no moat yet.** "The models are rented; the memory is owned" is true only after months of daily use, and there are zero onboarded shops. Until memory accumulates, a copycat, or Khatabook/OkCredit adding voice capture on their existing 50M+ installs, can match the product. The first-mover edge is thin and the brief gives no evidence the accuracy is 10x better than anything else. Who sells this within reach of the same buyer today is unmeasured.
2. **Distribution is unproven and could be the whole business.** In-person, one cluster at a time, is slow and costs money per shop. There is no CAC, no cluster density, no market size. A free operations layer means every shop must be acquired before any of them pays. A product with no cheap way to reach buyers is not yet a business.
3. **The monopoly and the money both depend on trust and a habit that do not exist yet.** The moat requires owners to open it every morning, and the revenue (udhaar fees, lending spread, take-rate) requires that habit plus customers who accept reminders and payment links in a credit culture that runs on relationships. Zero retention data, no price, no cost-to-serve, and no team described. Rented models also mean margin and capability depend on suppliers who can reprice or compete.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about how a kirana runs that Khatabook, OkCredit and the AI call-bots do not?
- Which shops, in which cluster, will you own first, and how many are there? (open: market size and cluster density, `/founder-competitors` and `/founder-consumer`)
- How do the first 100 shops arrive, and what does each cost? (open: CAC, `/founder-consumer`)
- What does it cost to serve one shop per month in AI and API spend, and what would you charge? (open: cost to serve and price, `/founder-cfo`)
- What stops a well-funded incumbent from shipping voice capture in month six? (open: who is already close, `/founder-competitors`)
- What is the retention of the first ten shops after 30, 60, 90 days? (open: no data yet, `/founder-consumer`)
- Which of the two durability routes (data switching cost, supplier network) do you expect to bite first, and what is the evidence? 
- Who is on the team, and why are they the right people for field sales plus voice AI?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- A pilot in one cluster with a real number of onboarded shops and 90-day retention that shows the morning-habit claim holds (a threshold to be agreed with the founder, not invented here).
- A measured CAC for in-person onboarding and a cost-to-serve figure, with a price or revenue path attached (`/founder-cfo`).
- A competitor map showing how close the ledger apps and call-bots are to voice capture, and a stated reason this lead survives them (`/founder-competitors`).
- Evidence that the accumulated-memory switching cost is real: shops that would lose something concrete by leaving.
- A described team with field-sales and voice-AI strength.

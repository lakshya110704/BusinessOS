# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One sentence is clear.** "Keep the human, remove the work": capture how the shopkeeper already talks, and build the books automatically. The pitch passes the one-sentence test.
- **The experience starts where the customer already is.** There is no new app and no new number. The owner stays in WhatsApp and on the phone. This removes the typing that the pitch says makes ledger apps go stale.
- **One model underneath.** "Everything is an order with a direction" lets stock, khata and buying patterns be computed instead of separately built. If it holds, this is real focus: one stream of data, many answers.
- **The confirm step shows care.** Nothing is logged without the owner's tap, and the repeat-order path has been made lighter. That respects both trust and effort.
- **The loop is built end to end** (per the hypothetical state), including the Hindi "ask your business" moment, which is the part an owner would feel.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It may be a feature the owner does not feel the need for daily.** The product is built and unused. Nothing shows the owner opens it every morning. The goal stated is a daily habit, but the experience may be passive: capture happens in the background and the owner is never pulled back. If the first five minutes do not return something the owner values that same day, the product becomes another stale ledger, with no typing needed to go stale.
2. **Seams in the capture chain.** The product spans phone calls, WhatsApp text, Hindi voice, confirm taps, and templates. Each handoff is a place for a wrong item, a missed call, or a confirm card that goes unnoticed in a busy shop. Accuracy on "real shop speech" is claimed, not shown. One wrong khata entry against a customer who owes money damages trust in the whole book.
3. **Scope is wider than one thing done brilliantly.** Orders, stock, khata, Q&A assistant, dashboard, collections, UPI links, credit and a buyer-supplier network are all in the pitch. The product lens asks what to cut. The one thing looks like "no-typing khata and order memory". Collections, credit and network are the business model, not the product, and they need a trusted book underneath first.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing the owner does with BusinessOS on day 1, day 7 and day 30, and why would they come back without being nudged?
- Walk me through the first five minutes after a shop signs up. What does the owner see that proves it works, with an empty history?
- What does the owner do when the AI is wrong, and how fast can they fix it? How is a wrong entry kept from reaching a customer's balance?
- What would you cut if you could only ship one of: Q&A assistant, derived stock, collections?
- Who does setup (importing the paper diary, the customer list, the item names), and is that part of the product or a hidden service?
- Open numbers, not to be invented: accuracy on real shop speech, share of orders captured vs missed, owner confirm rate and time-to-confirm. Which skill answers: /founder-consumer (what owners will actually do and pay attention to).
- Open numbers: cost to serve one shop per month (AI, voice, call ingestion). Which skill answers: /founder-cfo.
- Open question: what do shopkeepers do today when they want the same result, and what do Khatabook, OkCredit and the AI call-bots offer in the same moment? Which skill answers: /founder-competitors.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- A small, supervised pilot of real shops before the money is released at scale, with weekly open rate, orders captured, and corrections per shop reported as product evidence (measured with /founder-consumer).
- A written definition of the one thing that must be brilliant (the no-typing order and khata memory) and a decision that collections, credit and network wait until that is shown to stick.
- A measured error rate on real calls and voice notes, with a named threshold below which a shop is not onboarded.
- A defined first-five-minutes experience that returns value on day 1 without historical data.
- Cost to serve per shop stated before pricing is set (/founder-cfo).
